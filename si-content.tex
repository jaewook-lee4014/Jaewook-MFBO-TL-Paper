\del{We provide implementation details for the multi-fidelity Gaussian process (MFGP) baseline.}\new{Supplementary Table~\ref{supptab:surrogate_config} lists, for each of the twelve surrogates as run, the fitting procedure, training length, learning rates, weight decay, input and target scaling, the representation on which the predictive model acts, the kernel and likelihood or the predictive head, the inducing points and the floating-point precision. \subsection{\del{Model configuration}\new{Baseline MFGP}}

We use \texttt{SingleTaskMultiFidelityGP} from BoTorch~\citep{balandat2020botorch}\new{ 0.10.0}, which
internally employs a Mat\'{e}rn 5/2 kernel with automatic relevance determination (ARD)

The inputs enter as they are, with the fidelity appended as a 0/1 column, and the targets of both fidelities are standardized together by the \texttt{Standardize} outcome transform; no input transform is applied}.

At each iteration the GP was retrained from scratch by maximizing the exact marginal
log-likelihood \new{(the posterior density under these priors) }with L-BFGS-B via \texttt{fit\_gpytorch\_mll}\new{ in double precision}, and predictions were taken
from the posterior at the high-fidelity (HF) level $s=1$\new{, whose standard deviation is that of the latent function}. The multi-fidelity GP uses this
single cross-fidelity posterior for acquisition at both fidelity steps\new{: EI with the HF incumbent selects the LF query and the lowest posterior mean selects the HF query (Methods)}.

\subsection{\new{Deep-kernel GP}}

\new{The deep-kernel GP is a GPyTorch exact GP whose kernel acts on the output of a feature network with layers of widths 64, 64 and 16 and $\tanh$ hidden activations, rescaled to $[-1, 1]$ by \texttt{ScaleToBounds}; the covariance is an RBF kernel with ARD over the 16 features multiplied by a two-level index kernel of rank 1 that carries the fidelity, with a constant mean and a Gaussian likelihood, all without priors. Feature network, kernel, mean, index kernel and likelihood are optimized jointly on the exact marginal likelihood by 500 full-batch Adam steps (learning rate $10^{-3}$ for the feature network and $10^{-2}$ for the GP parameters) in single precision, without weight decay. Inputs and targets enter as they are, and the predictive distribution includes the learned observation noise.}

\subsection{\new{Sparse variational MFGP}}

\new{The sparse variational MFGP is a GPyTorch approximate GP with a Cholesky variational distribution over $\min(100, N)$ inducing points, initialized as a random subset of the $N$ training inputs of both fidelities and optimized together with the hyperparameters; the covariance is an RBF kernel with ARD over the $d$ inputs multiplied by the same two-level index kernel, with a constant mean and a Gaussian likelihood, without priors. All parameters are optimized on the variational evidence lower bound over the full training set (no mini-batching) by 500 Adam steps at learning rate $10^{-2}$ in single precision, without weight decay. The number of inducing points equals the training-set size until it reaches 100 and stays at 100 thereafter. Inputs and targets enter as they are, and the predictive distribution includes the learned observation noise. Repeated fits of this model with the same seed are not bit-identical (Methods).}


\suppnote{Uncertainty quantification via Bayesian linear regression}{suppnote:blr}
Uncertainty for the transfer-learning surrogates is obtained from a Bayesian linear regression (BLR) head on the low-fidelity (LF) network's last-layer features, following DNGO~\citep{snoek2015scalable}. Let $\varphi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^D$
denote the penultimate-layer representation and $\tilde{\varphi}(x) = [\varphi(x), 1]$ the
augmented feature vector, with a Gaussian prior over the weights $w$:
\begin{equation}
y \mid x \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\tilde{\varphi}(x)^\top w,\, \beta^{-1}\right), \qquad w \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\, \alpha^{-1} I\right),
\end{equation}
with prior precision $\alpha$ and noise precision $\beta$. The closed-form posterior
yields predictive moments
\begin{equation}
\mu(x) = \tilde{\varphi}(x)^\top m_N, \qquad \sigma^2(x) = \beta^{-1} + \tilde{\varphi}(x)^\top S_N\, \tilde{\varphi}(x),
\end{equation}
where $(m_N, S_N)$ are the posterior mean and covariance\del{ and we set $\alpha = \beta = 1$}. \del{In the primary configuration the LF expected improvement (EI) uses a constant predictive standard deviation, so the BLR head contributes a regularized posterior mean and the acquisition reduces to greedy selection; the five-acquisition uncertainty-driven portfolio that instead uses the BLR predictive standard deviation, and an optional HF BLR head with EI on both fidelities, are evaluated in Supplementary Note~5.}\new{Two such heads are used. The LF head, with $\alpha = \beta = 1$, gives the LF predictive distribution on which the calibration analysis of Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration} is based. The HF head supplies the HF prediction (Methods): it regresses the HF residuals $y^{(H)} - g^{(L)}_{\theta}(x)$ on $[\,g^{(L)}_{\theta}(x),\, \varphi^{(L)}(x),\, 1\,]$, with the ratio of prior to noise precision of the representation block chosen by leave-one-out error over $\{10, 30, 100, 300, 1000\}$, ratios of $10$ and $1$ for the $g^{(L)}_{\theta}$ coefficient and the bias, and the noise precision set to the inverse of the leave-one-out mean squared error, clipped to $[1, 100]$; with fewer than four HF observations the leave-one-out selection is skipped and the representation block keeps a prior precision of 100 with $\beta = 10$. Under the primary greedy policy both fidelities are queried by the HF predictive mean $\mu^{(H)}$; the five-acquisition portfolio of Supplementary Note~\ref{suppnote:acquisition_variants} instead selects the LF query from the HF predictive mean and standard deviation of this head, using the HF incumbent.}

\label{suppsec:training_procedures}
\del{All models use $\ell_2$ weight decay (WD=1e-4).}\new{Weight decay ($\ell_2$, 1e-4) is applied by Sequential, Stop-Gradient Joint and End-to-End Joint; the other configurations use none.} \emph{Sequential}: the LF network is

\emph{Progressive}: Stage 1 trains only the HF \del{head}\new{output layer} with the backbone frozen; Stage 2

fidelities at a reduced learning rate\new{ with loss $0.3\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{LF}} + 0.7\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{HF}}$}. \emph{Stop-Gradient Joint}: joint training with

\paragraph{Scharber proxy for HOPV15.}
\new{The LF value of each donor is the Scharber-model power conversion efficiency~\citep{scharber2006design},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{OC}} &= \max\{0,\; -E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} - E^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\mathrm{LUMO}} - \Delta\}, &\quad E^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\mathrm{LUMO}} &= 4.3~\mathrm{eV}~(\mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}),\; \Delta = 0.3~\mathrm{V},\\
J_{\mathrm{SC}} &= \mathrm{EQE}\cdot J_{\max}(E_g), &\quad \mathrm{EQE} &= 0.65,\\
\mathrm{PCE}\,(\%) &= 100\, V_{\mathrm{OC}}\, J_{\mathrm{SC}}\,\mathrm{FF}/P_{\mathrm{in}}, &\quad \mathrm{FF} &= 0.65,\; P_{\mathrm{in}} = 100~\mathrm{mW\,cm^{-2}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $J_{\max}(E_g)$ is the short-circuit current density available from the AM1.5G spectrum for photon energies above $E_g$, tabulated at 19 gap values between $0.5$ and $3.0$~eV ($65$ to $1.8$~mA\,cm$^{-2}$) and interpolated linearly (clamped outside the table). $E_{\mathrm{HOMO}}$ and $E_g$ are the experimentally reported values of the HOPV15 experimental block (electrochemical HOMO level; optical gap), not computed orbital energies; the gap is taken from, in order, the optical gap ($194$ molecules), the electrochemical gap ($46$) and the LUMO--HOMO difference ($0$). Molecules lacking a HOMO level, any gap or a PCE were dropped ($350 \to 240$; no SMILES failed to parse). The PC$_{61}$BM acceptor level is applied to every donor although the reported devices used PC$_{71}$BM ($123$ molecules), PC$_{61}$BM ($101$) or other acceptors ($16$). The clamp at $V_{\mathrm{OC}} = 0$ is never active (LF minimum $0.42\%$); the LF ranges from $0.42$ to $14.43\%$ and the HF from $0.008$ to $10.2\%$.}

\paragraph{Band-gap target and elastic-modulus pools.}
\new{Both fidelities of the two band-gap pools are replaced by $|E_g - 1.4~\mathrm{eV}|$ before optimization, so that the $R^2$ of Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks} is computed on the transformed values. The elastic-modulus pools are used on the linear GPa scale, negated, after removing the compounds without a finite, positive modulus at either fidelity.}

\paragraph{Cost ratios.}
\new{The cost ratios of the synthetic functions ($0.1$ / $0.5$), COFs ($0.065$), FreeSolv ($0.1$) and polarizability ($0.167$) are inherited from the studies that introduced the pools~\citep{sabanza2025best,gantzler2023multifidelity,fare2022multi}. The remaining values are assumptions of this work: HOPV15 uses $0.1$ by analogy with FreeSolv, because the Scharber proxy is a closed-form estimate from measured quantities rather than a calculation; Matbench-gap uses $0.05$, chosen so that the round-robin schedule issues $20$ LF queries per HF query, whereas a literal PBE-to-experiment cost ratio would be far smaller and would give hundreds of LF queries per HF query; ExptGap-PBE and the three elastic-modulus pools use $0.05$ by the same convention, although an MLIP elastic calculation costs roughly $10^{-3}$ of a DFT one. Because cost is charged per query, $\rho$ is a schedule parameter rather than a measured cost.}

\new{The remaining subsections of this note define the two synthetic functions and their scenarios. }Both synthetic functions are standard multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization (MFBO) benchmarks adopted from~Sabanza-Gil et
al.~\citep{sabanza2025best}.

\subsection{Branin function}
The HF Branin function is
\begin{equation}
f^{(H)}(x_1, x_2) = \left(x_2 - b x_1^2 + c x_1 - 6\right)^2 + 10\left(1 - \frac{1}{8\pi}\right)\cos(x_1) + 10,
\end{equation}
with $b = 5.1/(4\pi^2)$, $c = 5/\pi$, domain $x_1 \in [-5, 10]$, $x_2 \in [0, 15]$, and
global minimum $f^* = 0.397887$. The LF approximation modifies only the
quadratic coefficient, $b_{\text{LF}} = b - 0.1(1-\alpha)$, so that the correlation
degrades as $\alpha$ decreases.

\subsection{Park function}
The HF Park function is
\begin{equation}
f^{(H)}(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) = \frac{x_1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{(x_2 + x_3^2)x_4}{x_1^2}} + (x_1 + 3x_4)\exp\!\left(1 + \sin(x_3)\right),
\end{equation}
with $x_i \in [0,1]$ and global minimum $f^* = 0$\new{, approached as $x_1, x_4 \to 0$. This is the form implemented in the released code and used to generate the candidate lattice; it differs from the classical Park function, $\tfrac{x_1}{2}\bigl[\sqrt{1 + (x_2 + x_3^2)x_4/x_1^2} - 1\bigr] + (x_1 + 3x_4)\exp(1 + \sin x_3)$, in the first term, and lattice points with $x_1 = 0$ or $x_4 = 0$ are evaluated with a $10^{-8}$ guard on $x_1$ and $x_4$}. The LF approximation modifies
the $x_4$ coefficient ($3 \rightarrow 3 - 1.5(1-\alpha)$) and scales the \del{exponential
argument}\new{sine term of the exponent} by $\alpha$\new{, $\exp(1 + \alpha\sin(x_3))$}.
