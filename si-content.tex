\suppnote{Surrogate configurations}{suppnote:mfgp}

Supplementary Table~\ref{supptab:surrogate_config} lists, for each of the eight surrogates as run, the fitting procedure, training length, learning rates, weight decay, input and target scaling, the representation on which the predictive model acts, the kernel and likelihood or the predictive head, the inducing points and the floating-point precision. The factors held fixed across all eight are the candidate pool and its standardized feature representation (Methods), the budget, cost ratio and fidelity schedule, the initial design (farthest-point sampling from the same seed), the retraining of the surrogate from scratch at every refit and the seeds. The five transfer-learning surrogates further share the backbone (two hidden layers of 64 $\tanh$ units), the optimizer (full-batch Adam, fixed epoch budgets, no early stopping), the pooled standardization of inputs and targets and the predictive head. The three GP baselines are the off-the-shelf configurations of their libraries (BoTorch 0.10.0, GPyTorch 1.11) and differ from the transfer-learning surrogates, and from one another, in fitting procedure, training length, scaling and precision; these differences are reported here rather than removed.

\subsection{Baseline MFGP}

We use \texttt{SingleTaskMultiFidelityGP} from BoTorch~\citep{balandat2020botorch} 0.10.0, which
internally employs a Mat\'{e}rn 5/2 kernel with automatic relevance determination (ARD)
for the input dimensions and a linear truncated kernel for the fidelity dimension, with the library's default priors: Gamma(3, 6) and Gamma(6, 2) on the lengthscales of the unbiased and biased Mat\'{e}rn components of the fidelity kernel, Gamma(3, 3) on its power, Gamma(2, 0.15) on the output scale and Gamma(1.1, 0.05) on the noise variance, which is constrained to at least $10^{-4}$; the mean is a learned constant. The inputs enter as they are, with the fidelity appended as a 0/1 column, and the targets of both fidelities are standardized together by the \texttt{Standardize} outcome transform; no input transform is applied.

At each iteration the GP was retrained from scratch by maximizing the exact marginal
log-likelihood (the posterior density under these priors) with L-BFGS-B via \texttt{fit\_gpytorch\_mll} in double precision, and predictions were taken
from the posterior at the high-fidelity (HF) level $s=1$, whose standard deviation is that of the latent function. The multi-fidelity GP uses this
single cross-fidelity posterior for acquisition at both fidelity steps: EI with the HF incumbent selects the LF query and the lowest posterior mean selects the HF query (Methods).

\subsection{Deep-kernel GP}

The deep-kernel GP is a GPyTorch exact GP whose kernel acts on the output of a feature network with layers of widths 64, 64 and 16 and $\tanh$ hidden activations, rescaled to $[-1, 1]$ by \texttt{ScaleToBounds}; the covariance is an RBF kernel with ARD over the 16 features multiplied by a two-level index kernel of rank 1 that carries the fidelity, with a constant mean and a Gaussian likelihood, all without priors. Feature network, kernel, mean, index kernel and likelihood are optimized jointly on the exact marginal likelihood by 500 full-batch Adam steps (learning rate $10^{-3}$ for the feature network and $10^{-2}$ for the GP parameters) in single precision, without weight decay. Inputs and targets enter as they are, and the predictive distribution includes the learned observation noise.

\subsection{Sparse variational MFGP}

The sparse variational MFGP is a GPyTorch approximate GP with a Cholesky variational distribution over $\min(100, N)$ inducing points, initialized as a random subset of the $N$ training inputs of both fidelities and optimized together with the hyperparameters; the covariance is an RBF kernel with ARD over the $d$ inputs multiplied by the same two-level index kernel, with a constant mean and a Gaussian likelihood, without priors. All parameters are optimized on the variational evidence lower bound over the full training set (no mini-batching) by 500 Adam steps at learning rate $10^{-2}$ in single precision, without weight decay. The number of inducing points equals the training-set size until it reaches 100 and stays at 100 thereafter. Inputs and targets enter as they are, and the predictive distribution includes the learned observation noise. Repeated fits of this model with the same seed are not bit-identical (Methods).


\suppnote{Uncertainty quantification via Bayesian linear regression}{suppnote:blr}
The HF prediction of the transfer-learning surrogates, its uncertainty and the LF predictive distribution are obtained from a Bayesian linear regression (BLR) head on the low-fidelity (LF) network's last-layer features, an adaptive-basis construction in the spirit of DNGO~\citep{snoek2015scalable}, with the difference that the basis is the representation of the LF network, learned from the abundant LF data, rather than that of a network trained on the target data; the residual network of Methods Eq.~(\ref{eq:residual}) enters the training loss only, and the closed-form head supplies the predictive mean and variance that the acquisition functions use. Let $\varphi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^D$
denote the penultimate-layer representation and $\tilde{\varphi}(x) = [\varphi(x), 1]$ the
augmented feature vector, with a Gaussian prior over the weights $w$:
\begin{equation}
y \mid x \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\tilde{\varphi}(x)^\top w,\, \beta^{-1}\right), \qquad w \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\, \alpha^{-1} I\right),
\end{equation}
with prior precision $\alpha$ and noise precision $\beta$. The closed-form posterior
yields predictive moments
\begin{equation}
\mu(x) = \tilde{\varphi}(x)^\top m_N, \qquad \sigma^2(x) = \beta^{-1} + \tilde{\varphi}(x)^\top S_N\, \tilde{\varphi}(x),
\end{equation}
where $(m_N, S_N)$ are the posterior mean and covariance. Two such heads are used. The LF head, with $\alpha = \beta = 1$, gives the LF predictive distribution on which the calibration analysis of Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration} is based. The HF head supplies the HF prediction (Methods): it regresses the HF residuals $y^{(H)} - g^{(L)}_{\theta}(x)$ on $[\,g^{(L)}_{\theta}(x),\, \varphi^{(L)}(x),\, 1\,]$, with the ratio of prior to noise precision of the representation block chosen, for at least four HF observations, by leave-one-out error over $\{10, 30, 100, 300, 1000\}$, ratios of $10$ and $1$ for the $g^{(L)}_{\theta}$ coefficient and the bias, and the noise precision set to the inverse of the leave-one-out mean squared error, clipped to $[1, 100]$; with fewer than four HF observations the leave-one-out selection is skipped and the representation block keeps a prior precision of 100 with $\beta = 10$. Under the primary greedy policy both fidelities are queried by the HF predictive mean $\mu^{(H)}$; the five-acquisition portfolio of Supplementary Note~\ref{suppnote:acquisition_variants} instead selects the LF query from the HF predictive mean and standard deviation of this head, using the HF incumbent. The head treats the representation as a fixed basis. In Pretrain-then-Joint, End-to-End Joint and Soft Parameter Sharing the HF loss also reaches the LF network, so the representation has been trained on the same HF values the head is then fitted to, and neither the leave-one-out rule nor the posterior covariance of the head accounts for that first fit; the HF predictive standard deviation of these three surrogates is therefore optimistic relative to the configurations whose representation the HF values never reach (in Domain Adaptation the alignment penalty passes the HF inputs, not the HF values, to the LF network). This concerns only the acquisition portfolio of Supplementary Note~\ref{suppnote:acquisition_variants}: the primary greedy policy uses the HF predictive mean alone, and the calibration analysis uses the LF head.

\subsection{Training procedures and method-specific parameters}
\label{suppsec:training_procedures}
Weight decay ($\ell_2$, 1e-4) is applied by Frozen-representation transfer and End-to-End Joint; the other three configurations use none. 
\emph{Frozen-representation transfer}: both networks are trained from scratch for 300 joint epochs on $0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{LF}} + 0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{HF}}$ (Adam, learning rate 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4), with the LF prediction that enters the residual network detached, so that no HF gradient reaches the LF network; the representation is shaped by the LF data alone and the HF data enter the prediction only through the BLR head of Supplementary Note~\ref{suppnote:blr}. For reproducibility, this is the \texttt{DNGOJoint} configuration of the released code, with the residual network trained but unused.
\emph{Pretrain-then-Joint}: Stage 1 pretrains on LF data; Stage 2 jointly trains on both
fidelities at a reduced learning rate with loss $0.3\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{LF}} + 0.7\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{HF}}$. \emph{End-to-End Joint}: joint training with full gradient flow (loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{LF}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{HF}}$; learning rates 1e-3 for the LF and 5e-4 for the HF network). \emph{Soft Parameter Sharing}: joint training on $0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{LF}} + 0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{HF}} + \lambda_{\text{soft}}\,\Omega$ with $\Omega = \bigl\|W^{(L)}_{1} - W^{(H)}_{1,\,\cdot\,,\,1:d}\bigr\|_F^2$, the squared Frobenius distance between the first-layer weight matrices of the two networks over the $d$ shared input columns; the column of the HF network that receives the LF prediction, the biases and the second and output layers are not penalized, $\lambda_{\text{soft}}=0.01$.
 \emph{Domain Adaptation (MMD)}: maximum-mean-discrepancy penalty $\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=0.1$ aligning LF and HF features (RBF kernel of bandwidth 1 between the hidden features of the LF network on the LF training inputs and of the HF network on the HF training inputs, the LF set truncated to its first $n_{\text{HF}}$ points), with both networks updated at learning rate 1e-4 in the HF stage; the LF loss is not part of this stage, so the LF network moves only through the penalty. Under the BLR head of Supplementary Note~\ref{suppnote:blr} the residual networks above are trained but do not enter the prediction, so what separates the five surrogates at prediction time is whether the HF data reach the representation of the LF network. Frozen-representation transfer predicts from a representation shaped by the LF data alone, its LF prediction being detached before it reaches the residual network; Pretrain-then-Joint, End-to-End Joint and Soft Parameter Sharing update that representation through the HF loss; and Domain Adaptation (MMD) updates it only through the alignment penalty, which sees the HF inputs but not the HF values. Supplementary Table~\ref{supptab:surrogate_config} summarizes these settings for all eight surrogates.

\paragraph{Scharber proxy for HOPV15.}
The LF value of each donor is the Scharber-model power conversion efficiency~\citep{scharber2006design},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{OC}} &= \max\{0,\; -E_{\mathrm{HOMO}} - E^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\mathrm{LUMO}} - \Delta\}, &\quad E^{\mathrm{acc}}_{\mathrm{LUMO}} &= 4.3~\mathrm{eV}~(\mathrm{PC}_{61}\mathrm{BM}),\; \Delta = 0.3~\mathrm{V},\\
J_{\mathrm{SC}} &= \mathrm{EQE}\cdot J_{\max}(E_g), &\quad \mathrm{EQE} &= 0.65,\\
\mathrm{PCE}\,(\%) &= 100\, V_{\mathrm{OC}}\, J_{\mathrm{SC}}\,\mathrm{FF}/P_{\mathrm{in}}, &\quad \mathrm{FF} &= 0.65,\; P_{\mathrm{in}} = 100~\mathrm{mW\,cm^{-2}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $J_{\max}(E_g)$ is the short-circuit current density available from the AM1.5G spectrum for photon energies above $E_g$, tabulated at 19 gap values between $0.5$ and $3.0$~eV ($65$ to $1.8$~mA\,cm$^{-2}$) and interpolated linearly (clamped outside the table). $E_{\mathrm{HOMO}}$ and $E_g$ are the experimentally reported values of the HOPV15 experimental block (electrochemical HOMO level; optical gap), not computed orbital energies; the gap is taken from, in order, the optical gap ($194$ molecules), the electrochemical gap ($46$) and the LUMO--HOMO difference ($0$). Molecules lacking a HOMO level, any gap or a PCE were dropped ($350 \to 240$; no SMILES failed to parse). The PC$_{61}$BM acceptor level is applied to every donor although the reported devices used PC$_{71}$BM ($123$ molecules), PC$_{61}$BM ($101$) or other acceptors ($16$). The clamp at $V_{\mathrm{OC}} = 0$ is never active (LF minimum $0.42\%$); the LF ranges from $0.42$ to $14.43\%$ and the HF from $0.008$ to $10.2\%$.

\paragraph{Band-gap target and elastic-modulus pools.}
Both fidelities of the two band-gap pools are replaced by $|E_g - 1.4~\mathrm{eV}|$ before optimization, so that the $R^2$ of Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks} is computed on the transformed values. The elastic-modulus pools are used on the linear GPa scale, negated, after removing the compounds without a finite, positive modulus at either fidelity.

\paragraph{Cost ratios.}
The cost ratios of the synthetic functions ($0.1$ / $0.5$), COFs ($0.065$), FreeSolv ($0.1$) and polarizability ($0.167$) are inherited from the studies that introduced the pools~\citep{sabanza2025best,gantzler2023multifidelity,fare2022multi}. The remaining values are assumptions of this work: HOPV15 uses $0.1$ by analogy with FreeSolv, because the Scharber proxy is a closed-form estimate from measured quantities rather than a calculation; Matbench-gap uses $0.05$, chosen so that the round-robin schedule issues $20$ LF queries per HF query, whereas a literal PBE-to-experiment cost ratio would be far smaller and would give hundreds of LF queries per HF query; ExptGap-PBE and the three elastic-modulus pools use $0.05$ by the same convention, although an MLIP elastic calculation costs roughly $10^{-3}$ of a DFT one. Because cost is charged per query, $\rho$ is a schedule parameter rather than a measured cost.

The remaining subsections of this note define the two synthetic functions and their scenarios. The synthetic functions follow the Branin and Park benchmark families used in multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization (MFBO) by Sabanza-Gil et
al.~\citep{sabanza2025best}; the implemented definitions are given below.

\subsection{Branin function}
The HF Branin function is
\begin{equation}
f^{(H)}(x_1, x_2) = \left(x_2 - b x_1^2 + c x_1 - 6\right)^2 + 10\left(1 - \frac{1}{8\pi}\right)\cos(x_1) + 10,
\end{equation}
with $b = 5.1/(4\pi^2)$, $c = 5/\pi$, domain $x_1 \in [-5, 10]$, $x_2 \in [0, 15]$, and
global minimum $f^* = 0.397887$. The LF approximation modifies only the
quadratic coefficient, $b_{\text{LF}} = b - 0.1(1-\alpha)$, so that the correlation
degrades as $\alpha$ decreases.

\subsection{Park function}
The HF Park function is
\begin{equation}
f^{(H)}(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) = \frac{\tilde{x}_1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{(x_2 + x_3^2)\tilde{x}_4}{\tilde{x}_1^2 + \epsilon}} + (\tilde{x}_1 + 3\tilde{x}_4)\exp\!\left(1 + \sin(x_3)\right),
\end{equation}
with $x_i \in [0,1]$, $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ and $\tilde{x}_j = \max(x_j,\epsilon)$ for $j=1,4$. The unregularized function has global minimum $f^* = 0$, approached as $x_1, x_4 \to 0$. This is the form implemented in the released code and used to generate the candidate lattice; it differs from the classical Park function, $\tfrac{x_1}{2}\bigl[\sqrt{1 + (x_2 + x_3^2)x_4/x_1^2} - 1\bigr] + (x_1 + 3x_4)\exp(1 + \sin x_3)$, in the first term, and the guards on $x_1$ and $x_4$ and the denominator regularization use $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$. The LF approximation modifies
the $x_4$ coefficient ($3 \rightarrow 3 - 1.5(1-\alpha)$) and scales the sine term of the exponent by $\alpha$, $\exp(1 + \alpha\sin(x_3))$.

\subsection{Scenario configurations}
Supplementary Table~\ref{supptab:synthetic_scenarios} lists the $\alpha$ values and resulting
characteristics. The $R^2$ values were computed by sampling 1,000 uniform points,
evaluating both fidelities, and fitting a linear regression from LF to HF, whose $R^2$ is the squared LF--HF Pearson correlation of Methods. Candidate pools
were constructed by uniform grid discretization ($50^2 = 2{,}500$ for Branin; $4^4 = 256$
for Park). The lattices include the domain boundaries, and regret is measured relative to the best candidate of the lattice rather than the analytical minimum: the best Branin lattice point has $f^{(H)} = 0.4045$ (analytical $f^* = 0.3979$) and the best Park lattice point $1.1 \times 10^{-7}$.

\suppnote{Acquisition variants and calibration}{suppnote:acquisition_variants}

This section specifies the protocol of the matched-acquisition and acquisition-portfolio comparisons summarized in the main text; their results are shown in Supplementary Figs.~\ref{suppfig:acquisition} and~\ref{suppfig:portfolio} and their aggregated result files are included in the code release.

\paragraph{Acquisition variants.} Beyond the primary greedy policy, we evaluated a
portfolio of five uncertainty-driven acquisitions spanning the standard taxonomy of myopic
acquisition functions~\citep{shahriari2015taking}: the improvement-based EI~\citep{jones1998efficient} and probability of
improvement~\citep{kushner1964new}; the optimistic Gaussian-process lower confidence
bound~\citep{srinivas2012information} ($\beta=2$); the information-theoretic max-value
entropy search~\citep{wang2017max} (Gumbel approximation, ten max-value samples); and
sampling-based Thompson sampling~\citep{russo2018tutorial}. Each replaces only the LF acquisition, evaluated on the HF predictive mean and BLR standard deviation of the HF head (Supplementary Note~\ref{suppnote:blr}) with the HF incumbent, with the surrogate, round-robin schedule, HF step, and seeds held fixed, and was run across all
five transfer-learning surrogates and nine benchmarks ($5\times 9\times 20$ seeds per
acquisition, seeds 42--61). For each acquisition and benchmark we report the fraction of surrogates on
which it beats the greedy policy and the mean paired difference in attainment: no acquisition raises the mean attainment by more than about $0.07$ ($+0.069$) on any benchmark (Thompson sampling on Branin-Unfav; the next largest gains are probability of improvement on HOPV15, $+0.048$, and on Branin-Fav, $+0.047$), Thompson sampling lowers it by $0.09$ on HOPV15 and $0.14$ on Matbench-gap, and the lower confidence bound and max-value entropy search lower it by $0.03$--$0.11$ on the Branin functions
(Supplementary Fig.~\ref{suppfig:portfolio}). 
As a matched control for the surrogate-versus-acquisition question, the baseline MFGP was also run
with a greedy policy that selects by its posterior mean, all other settings held fixed
(Supplementary Fig.~\ref{suppfig:acquisition}).

\suppnote{Computational cost: absolute FLOPs and compute ranking}{suppnote:computational_cost}

Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling_law} establishes how surrogate compute scales with problem size;
here we report the absolute cost that underlies those laws. Each profile uses the median over seeds 42--43 at training-set fractions $0.1$, $0.3$, $0.5$, $0.7$ and $1.0$. The total floating-point operations (FLOPs) of the
